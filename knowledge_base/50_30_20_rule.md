# The 50/30/20 budgeting rule

## What it is
The 50/30/20 rule splits take-home income into three buckets. About 50% goes to needs: rent, groceries, utilities, commuting, insurance and basic health costs. About 30% goes to wants: eating out, food delivery, shopping, entertainment and subscriptions. At least 20% goes to savings and investments, such as an emergency fund, SIPs or paying down debt early.

## How to use it
Add up a typical month of spending by category and compare each bucket with the guideline. If wants are above 30%, that is usually the easiest place to cut, because these costs are flexible. If needs are above 50%, the fix is slower: a cheaper rental, a different commute, or renegotiating bills.

## Adapting it
The split is a starting point, not a law. In expensive cities rent alone can take 30% or more of income, so a 60/20/20 split may be more realistic. Students and early-career earners may save less than 20% at first; the habit matters more than the exact share. Treat the rule as a way to notice when one bucket is crowding out the others.

## Quick check
Savings rate = (income - expenses) / income. A savings rate below 20% for several months in a row is a signal to review wants first.

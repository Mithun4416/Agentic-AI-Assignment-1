# Emergency fund basics

## Why it matters
An emergency fund is money kept aside for surprises: a job loss, a medical bill, an urgent trip home or a laptop that dies before a deadline. Without it, people fall back on credit cards or personal loans, which turn a one-off shock into months of interest payments.

## How much
A common target is three to six months of essential expenses: rent, groceries, utilities, commuting and minimum loan payments. Freelancers and people with irregular income should aim for the upper end. Start smaller if needed: one month of essentials is a useful first milestone.

## Where to keep it
Keep it somewhere safe and easy to reach within a day or two, such as a separate savings account or a sweep-in fixed deposit. Avoid keeping it in volatile investments, because emergencies tend to arrive when markets are down.

## Building it
Automate a fixed transfer on salary day so the money moves before it can be spent. Redirect windfalls such as bonuses, refunds and gifts. Money freed up by cutting subscriptions or food delivery is a natural source: cutting a little from wants every month adds up to a meaningful buffer within a year.

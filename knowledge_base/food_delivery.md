# Reducing food delivery and eating-out spend

## Why it grows quietly
Food delivery apps make each order feel small, but delivery fees, platform fees, surge pricing and tips add a large share on top of the menu price. Orders also tend to cluster around stress, late nights and busy work weeks, so spending can rise sharply in one month without any single big purchase.

## Practical ways to cut it
- Set a monthly food-delivery limit, for example your recent average minus a fifth, and check it weekly.
- Pick two or three "delivery nights" a week instead of ordering by default.
- Cook in batches on weekends so there is always something ready on weekdays.
- Keep quick meals at home: eggs, dosa batter, instant oats, frozen parathas, fruit.
- Move delivery apps off the home screen; the extra friction reduces impulse orders.
- Use pickup instead of delivery when the restaurant is close.
- Turn off promotional notifications. Discounts mostly bring forward orders you would not have made.

## Measuring progress
Track the number of orders and the average order value separately. Reducing how often you order usually saves more than switching to cheaper restaurants.
